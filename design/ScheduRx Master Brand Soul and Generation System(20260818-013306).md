# 3. AUDIENCE PSYCHOLOGY

## Primary decision-maker

The primary buyer is an independent clinic owner or practicing doctor in India.

Typical characteristics:

- approximately 35–60 years old;
- clinically capable but time-constrained;
- responsible for both patient care and the business of the clinic;
- already using some combination of paper, calls, WhatsApp, spreadsheets, reception staff, and personal memory;
- frustrated by missed calls, no-shows, lost follow-ups, scattered patient information, and front-desk overload;
- skeptical of complicated enterprise software;
- unwilling to spend weeks learning a system;
- attracted to modernity but cautious about unreliable technology;
- concerned about trust, data, control, and patient experience;
- wants growth without turning the clinic into a corporate hospital;
- and wants technology to reduce mental load rather than create another dashboard to manage.

### What this audience needs to feel

“This understands how my clinic actually works.”

“This looks premium, but it will not be difficult.”

“This will make my clinic more professional.”

“I remain in control.”

“This will not embarrass me in front of my patients or staff.”

“This is reliable enough to become part of my daily practice.”

“This could save time and recover revenue without making care impersonal.”

The receptionist or front-desk operator needs:

- clarity;
- speed;
- visible current status;
- easy patient search;
- easy booking and rescheduling;
- a calm queue;
- fewer repeated tasks;

## Patient-facing audience

Patients should perceive the clinic as:

- responsive;
- organized;
- credible;
- modern;
- respectful;
- easy to reach;

## Investor audience

Investor-facing work should communicate:

- workflow depth;

# 7. ATMOSPHERIC LIGHT

The ScheduRx gradient is not a conventional linear gradient.

It should feel like **warm light passing through soft material**.

The transition may pass through:

- warm gray;
- taupe;
- cream;
- beige;
- pale peach;
- vivid orange;
- and diffused white.

There must be no hard gradient bands.

## Construction guidance

Build atmospheric fields with:

- one warm stone foundation;
- one or two oversized radial orange fields;
- one cream or white haze;
- blur between 24px and 70px depending on scale;
- broad falloff;
- controlled opacity;
- optional extremely subtle grain at 1–2% opacity.

Example visual logic:

```css
background:
  radial-gradient(
    ellipse at 74% 22%,
    rgba(236, 107, 37, 0.98) 0%,
    rgba(236, 107, 37, 0.76) 28%,
    rgba(236, 107, 37, 0.28) 54%,
    rgba(236, 107, 37, 0) 76%
  ),
  radial-gradient(
    ellipse at 28% 76%,
    rgba(255, 247, 239, 0.88) 0%,
    rgba(255, 247, 239, 0) 64%
  ),
  #B9B6B1;
```

Do not repeat one identical light field across every composition.

Create variations such as:

- orange-right;
- orange-center;
- orange-top;
- cream-wash;
- smoke;
- dark-warm;
- subtle-stone;
- live-state.

## Motion rule

Atmospheric light is usually static.

When motion is justified:

- move the light source only a few percentage points;
- use 8–15 second durations;
- keep opacity changes subtle;
- avoid visible blob wandering;
- avoid constant hue rotation;
- respect reduced-motion preferences.

No rainbow auroras.

No purple-blue AI glow.

No spinning conic gradients.

# 8. TYPOGRAPHY

Typography carries a large portion of the brand identity.

## Font direction

Preferred system:

```css
font-family:
  -apple-system,
  BlinkMacSystemFont,
  "SF Pro Display",
  "SF Pro Text",
  "Geist",
  "Helvetica Neue",
  Arial,
  sans-serif;
```

Do not distribute or depend on proprietary font files.

Geist is an appropriate fallback.

The visual character should be:

- clean;
- contemporary;
- light;
- human;
- neutral;
- exceptionally legible;
- and slightly editorial.

## Display typography

Use scale before weight.

Large display type:

- weight: 300–400;
- line height: 0.92–1.02;
- letter spacing: `-0.035em` to `-0.06em`;
- balanced wrapping;
- strong optical alignment.

## Suggested sizes

### Marketing display

Desktop:

- 72–112px;
- use the upper range only when the composition has substantial negative space.

Mobile:

- 48–68px;
- use only one truly oversized typographic moment.

### Product hero

Desktop:

- 56–84px.

Mobile around 390px:

- 44–58px.

### Large metric

Desktop:

- 64–104px.

Mobile:

- 54–82px.

Use tabular numerals for changing operational data.

### Page heading

- 36–56px desktop;
- 34–48px mobile;
- weight 300–400.

### Section heading

- 24–32px;
- weight 400;
- letter spacing around `-0.025em`.

### Body

- 16–18px;
- weight 400;
- line height 1.45–1.6.

### Metadata

- 13–14px;
- weight 400;
- never so pale that it becomes difficult to read.

## Mature-audience legibility rule

Feedback that “elements feel too big” must not be answered by shrinking the entire brand.

Instead:

- permit only one oversized object per viewport;
- keep operational body copy at 16px or above;
- never use weight 300 below approximately 26–28px;
- retain visible labels beside unfamiliar icons;
- keep line lengths comfortable;
- allow interface zoom up to 150%;
- avoid placing several huge numbers or headings beside each other;
- use hierarchy rather than compression;
- and maintain sufficient text contrast.

The experience should remain expressive for younger audiences and immediately understandable to doctors in their 40s, 50s, and 60s.

## Capitalization

Use sentence case.

Avoid excessive uppercase and tracking.

Uppercase may appear only in tiny editorial eyebrow labels when it serves a clear purpose.

# 9. LAYOUT AND COMPOSITION

## Primary rule

**One screen equals one dominant moment.**

Every composition must answer:

“What should the eye see first?”

If the answer is unclear, redesign the composition.

## Visual hierarchy

Use this hierarchy:

1. one dominant idea, metric, person, or action;
2. one supporting layer;
3. quiet operational detail;
4. progressive disclosure for secondary information.

Do not give every module equal visual weight.

## Negative space

Negative space is an active brand ingredient.

Do not automatically fill empty areas.

A large greeting and one meaningful clinic metric may occupy most of the first viewport.

## Mobile geometry

Primary design width:

- 390px first.

Also validate:

- 320px;
- 360px;
- 375px;
- 393px;
- 412px;
- 430px.

Mobile horizontal gutter:

- 18–22px.

Major section gap:

- 32–48px.

Large editorial transitions may use:

- 56–80px.

Minimum important touch target:

- 44×44px.

Preferred primary touch target:

- 48–56px.

## Desktop geometry

Use:

- a centered maximum content width around 1440–1480px;
- 24–32px outer padding;
- a 12-column system;
- asymmetric 7/5, 8/4, or 5/7 compositions;
- large gutters;
- and intentional open regions.

Do not stretch a mobile layout across desktop.

Do not use a grid of equal dashboard cards as the default desktop solution.

## Controlled asymmetry

Use:

- off-center light sources;
- unequal column widths;
- offset circles;
- overlapping controls;
- asymmetric text widths;
- varied module heights;
- and editorial whitespace.

The underlying alignment must remain precise.

Designed asymmetry is not randomness.

# 10. SHAPE LANGUAGE

## Radius system

- Small control: 18px
- Field: 24px
- Card: 28–32px
- Panel: 36–40px
- Hero: 42–48px
- Pill: 999px

Do not use one identical radius everywhere.

The larger and more architectural the surface, the larger its radius should feel.

## Circular motif

Circles are a ScheduRx signature.

Use circular controls for:

- arrows;
- next;
- back;
- close;
- call;
- capture;
- record;
- notifications;
- menu;
- microphone;
- send;
- add;
- compact AI actions;
- and important contextual navigation.

Recommended sizes:

- 44px;
- 48px;
- 52px;
- 56px.

Avoid tiny 28–32px icon buttons for important actions.

## Overlap

Permit controlled overlap where it communicates depth or connection.

Examples:

- an arrow circle overlapping the edge of a card;
- an avatar entering a hero surface;
- an action control floating between two material layers;
- a dark dock sitting slightly over content;
- a status pill bridging an image and an information panel.

Overlap should usually be 8–24px, not arbitrary.

```css
background: rgba(24, 24, 24, 0.48);
border: 1px solid rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.12);
backdrop-filter: blur(26px) saturate(105%);
```

## Glass rule

Glass is not a default card treatment.

Use glass only for objects that conceptually float:

- bottom docks;
- immersive encounter controls;
- AI overlays;
- live-call controls;
- modal chrome;
- hero search fields;
- floating action groups;
- and temporary context surfaces.

Normal data and content should usually use solid material.

Never stack glass inside glass.

## Borders

Use borders sparingly.

Light border:

```css
border: 1px solid rgba(24, 24, 24, 0.055);
```

Dark border:

```css
border: 1px solid rgba(255, 255, 255, 0.10);
```

Hierarchy should primarily come from:

- spacing;
- material;
- shape;
- typography;
- and subtle depth.

## Shadows

Shadows must be broad and quiet.

Card:

```css
box-shadow:
  0 1px 2px rgba(24, 24, 24, 0.015),
  0 12px 40px rgba(24, 24, 24, 0.055);
```

Floating control:

```css
box-shadow:
  0 12px 34px rgba(24, 24, 24, 0.12);
```

Avoid harsh small drop shadows.

Avoid obvious black elevation.

# 19. MOTION LANGUAGE

Motion should explain space and state.

## Timing

- press feedback: 100–140ms;
- micro-transition: 160–220ms;
- standard transition: 240–320ms;
- sheet: 320–420ms;
- page-level transition: 300–420ms.

## Easing

Use:

```css
cubic-bezier(.22, 1, .36, 1)
```

or an equivalent restrained spring.

## Appropriate motion

Use motion to show:

- a sheet emerging from below;
- a current-patient object expanding into encounter mode;
- the AI action becoming an assistant surface;
- a list row opening into detail;
- a queue patient changing;
- simple mode revealing advanced detail;
- a new appointment entering the timeline;
- and a message being sent.

## Press feedback

A subtle scale to approximately `0.975` is appropriate for important touch surfaces.

Do not bounce every element.

## Live states

Live or listening states may intensify orange light slightly.

Processing may use a slow warm shimmer.

Completion should return to calm.

Do not use rainbow listening visuals.

## Reduced motion

Respect reduced-motion settings.

Remove:

- unnecessary translation;
- continuous glow motion;
- pulsing;
- spring excess;
- and decorative animation.

State change must remain understandable without motion.
